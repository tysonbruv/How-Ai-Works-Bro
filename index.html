What is this thing?

This is the simple help screen.
Main goal:
The goal is to show how a tiny language model works. You give it example text. It learns little word patterns from that text. Then you give it a starting line, and it tries to continue the sentence.

It is basically: feed it words → train it → give it a start → it guesses more words.
!

The blue box is the toy model's pretend internet

On the main screen, just think: blue box = internet. But inside that pretend internet, imagine all the stuff AI can learn patterns from: books, conversations, sentences, code, lyrics, articles, examples, and training data.

When the blue box is empty, the tiny model has nothing because you have not given it words or sentences yet. It only starts learning once you start filling it with stuff.

1

Blue box = food for the model

Paste text into the big blue box. This is what the model learns from. More text gives it more patterns. Tiny text means tiny brain.

2

Purple box = settings

You can ignore this at first. Leave it on 2 words. That means the model looks at the last 2 words and guesses what word usually comes next.

3

Green button = make it learn

After you paste or change the blue text, click Train Model. This is like saying: “read this and remember the word patterns.”

4

Yellow box = starting words

Type the start of a sentence here. Example: “The dog”. The model uses those words as the starting point.

5

Orange box = answer comes out here

Click Train + Generate. The model will spit out words in the orange box. It might sound weird because it is not truly thinking. It is only guessing from patterns.

Dead simple example:
Blue box says: The dog ran home. The dog ate food.
Yellow prompt says: The dog
Output might say: The dog ran home. or The dog ate food.

That is the whole point. It learns what usually comes after words.
Important tiny-brain detail:
Also, human and humans are treated as different words. The tiny model does not know they mean almost the same thing.

When this page says it is not a big AI brain like ChatGPT, it means: this tiny model does not understand meaning, grammar, emotion, logic, or real-world facts. It only counts word patterns from the blue box and guesses what word might come next.

Extra tip: how this toy connects to ChatGPT

Bro version: ChatGPT is basically the mega version of this little Language Pattern toy.

This toy model does this:

“I saw human can before, so maybe the next word is fly.”

ChatGPT does a much more advanced version of this:

“Given everything in this conversation, what text should come next?”

But instead of remembering simple word pairs, ChatGPT has a huge neural network with billions of learned number-values inside it. Those numbers are called weights. Think of weights like tiny dials that get adjusted during training.

AI music works in a similar way

Think of AI generative music apps the same way.

This toy asks:

“What word usually comes next after these words?”

An AI music model is doing a similar idea, but with sound:

“What sound usually comes next after this sound?”
“What guitar note usually comes next after this guitar note?”
“What drum hit usually comes next after this beat?”
“What chord usually comes next after this chord?”
“What vocal texture usually comes next after this vocal line?”

So when an AI music app creates music, it is not sitting there like a human with a guitar thinking, “I feel sad, so I’ll write this beautiful chord.”

It has learned huge patterns from music/audio, like:

  • kick usually lands here
  • snare usually lands there
  • bass often follows the chord
  • a vocal line often rises before a chorus
  • a drop usually comes after a build-up
  • sad acoustic songs often use softer notes and space
  • house music often repeats grooves and slowly changes layers
Music AI version: “Given all the sound so far, what sound should come next?”

Text AI predicts text patterns. Music AI predicts sound patterns. Bigger models just do it at a much crazier level.

Now how they trained language, simple version

1. Feed it a stupid amount of text

They train a base model on huge amounts of text written by humans: books, websites, code, articles, conversations, and other text sources.

The basic training game is:

  1. Hide the next word/token.
  2. Ask the model to guess it.
  3. If it guesses wrong, adjust the internal number-dials slightly.
  4. Do that billions or trillions of times.
Example: The dog jumped over the ___
It guesses: fence
Maybe the real answer was: gate
So it slightly adjusts its internal dials.

Again and again and again. That is the base language model.

2. Then they teach it to follow instructions

A raw base model is not automatically a good assistant. It might ramble, complete your sentence weirdly, or answer in a useless way.

So humans give example prompts and write good answers. The model gets trained on examples like:

User asks question → assistant gives helpful answer.

3. Then humans compare answers

They show human reviewers multiple answers from the model and ask:

Which answer is better?

Example:

  • User asks: Explain tax deductions simply.
  • Answer A is confusing.
  • Answer B is clear.
  • Human says B is better.

They collect heaps of these comparisons.

4. Then they train a reward model

This part is wild. They train another model to predict what humans would prefer.

So now the system has something like a taste judge:

“This answer is probably better.”
“This answer is probably worse.”

5. Then they use RLHF

RLHF means Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback.

Simple version:

  • Reward the model when it gives answers humans prefer.
  • Push it away from answers humans dislike.

So what is ChatGPT “thinking”?

It is not thinking like a human with feelings and a soul sitting inside the computer. It is more like a massive prediction engine.

But because it was trained on so much text, code, explanations, arguments, questions, answers, stories, maths, and human conversation, the next-token prediction becomes crazy powerful.

At small scale, like this HTML toy file app you're using right now, it can say cooked stuff like:

human truck sky human can roll

At huge scale, it starts learning patterns like:

  • how grammar works
  • how code works
  • how questions work
  • how explanations work
  • how humans usually reason
  • how conversations flow
  • how tone works
  • how dog and dogs are connected
  • how human and humans are related

That is why this HTML toy is not a big AI brain like ChatGPT.

This HTML toy = a calculator with a notebook.
ChatGPT = a giant trained neural network running on serious computer hardware.

Dumbest possible summary

  1. Read internet-scale text.
  2. Guess missing words/tokens.
  3. Get corrected.
  4. Do that an insane number of times.
  5. Then humans teach it how to answer nicely.
  6. Then humans rank its answers.
  7. Then it gets tuned toward the answers humans prefer.

So this little Word Pattern / What Usually Comes Next toy model is the baby version of the first idea.

ChatGPT is that idea scaled up to an insane level, with human training layered on top.

Same idea, different AI types

  • Language - What usually comes next after this word, sentence, question, or conversation?
    Example:
    User types: The dog jumped over the

    It predicts:
    fence, gate, wall, or whatever usually comes next in writing.
  • Music - What usually comes next after this sound, chord, drum hit, vocal line, or moment in the song?
    Example:
    The song has a build-up, rising synth, faster drums, and tension.

    It predicts:
    drop, bass hit, kick drum, wider chorus, or a vocal hook.
  • Coding - What usually comes next after this code, function, file, error message, or programming pattern?
    Example:
    Code says: function add(a, b) { return

    It predicts:
    a + b;
  • Images - What pixels, shapes, colours, shadows, faces, objects, or background usually belong here?
    Example:
    Prompt says: a dog wearing sunglasses on a beach

    It predicts:
    dog shape, fur, sunglasses over the eyes, sand, sky, ocean, sunlight, shadows.
  • Video - What frame, movement, camera angle, or action usually comes next?
    Example:
    A video shows a person bending their knees before jumping.

    It predicts:
    body moves upward, feet leave ground, arms move, then the person lands.
  • Voice / Speech - What sound, word, tone, or voice pattern usually comes next?
    Example:
    Audio says: Hello, how are...

    It predicts:
    you, because that sound/phrase usually finishes that way.
  • Robotics - What action should usually happen next in this situation?
    Example:
    robot sees cup on table, hand is nearby

    It predicts:
    move hand toward cup, grip cup, lift cup
  • Recommendations - What would this person probably want, click, watch, buy, or listen to next?
    Example:
    Someone keeps watching Hilux videos, mowing business videos, and motorbike videos.

    It predicts:
    show them another ute video, mower setup video, tool review, or motorbike clip.
  • Gaming AI - What move, attack, dodge, path, or decision usually makes sense next?
    Example:
    Enemy sees player nearby, enemy has low health, player is aiming at them.

    It predicts:
    run behind cover, dodge, reload, attack, or retreat.
  • Medical AI - What condition, scan pattern, or warning sign does this usually look like?
    Example:
    A scan has a certain shadow, shape, size, and location that doctors have labelled before.

    It predicts:
    this pattern may match a known condition, or it may need a doctor to check it.
  • Fraud detection AI - Does this behaviour look normal, wrong, fake, risky, or suspicious?
    Example:
    Card is used in Sydney, then 10 minutes later it is used overseas.

    It predicts:
    suspicious pattern, possible fraud, block transaction or ask for verification.
  • Detection AI - What category does this thing belong in, and does anything look off?
    Example:
    Camera sees an object on the road.

    It predicts:
    person, dog, car, rubbish bag, traffic cone, or “unknown object”.
  • Blah - What usually comes after this part
  • Blah - What usually comes next after this
  • So on and so on
